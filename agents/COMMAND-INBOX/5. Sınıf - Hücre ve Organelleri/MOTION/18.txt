The clip opens with the fluid still carrying what has just crossed it: the dense granule sinks slowly through the cytoplasm and the wake it dragged hangs behind and above it, a curling thread of thicker jelly holding the light. Then the granule settles into the deeper current and slows — at that instant the thread straightens, loosens and begins folding back into the jelly around it, and the suspended dust it was holding disperses sideways into the flow. The clip begins with the cell looking like open space and ends with that space proved thick. The vacuole's bright rim shivers where the flow reaches it and dust-fine granules drift across the column of light rising from below. Camera: a shallow orbit swings right around the sinking granule at its own level, the near shoulder of the vacuole sweeping huge and unfocused through the lower frame so the wake turns in depth as the angle changes, then eases to a stop. The wake is a denser thread of the same jelly for the whole clip — it thins and rejoins the fluid and never becomes a drawn line, ribbon, arrow or motion streak. Silent clip, no audio, no dialogue. No whip-pan, no shake, no snap-zoom, no camera warp.
