═══ KOPYALA ═══

Warm instrumental Turkish folk-orchestral underscore for a children's educational film about
shared culture and belonging, no vocals at any point. Begins gently with a solo bağlama picking a
simple modal figure over soft sustained strings, an unhurried heartbeat pulse underneath, open and
inviting. A ney and a soft kanun answer the bağlama and the theme grows warm and communal, low
frame drum keeping a walking pace, never busy, always leaving space for a speaking voice above it.
Halfway through the piece darkens with restraint: the drum thins to a single low pulse, cello and
low strings carry a slower version of the same melody, the bağlama drops out, dignified and heavy
without becoming sad or dramatic. It stays quiet and patient there, sparse and respectful. Then it
lifts back: the frame drum returns, the bağlama comes in over the strings again with the opening
figure, and the ensemble opens into a warm hopeful full statement of the theme, generous but never
triumphant, no cymbal crashes and no big cinematic hit. It closes by thinning back to solo bağlama
and one held string chord that fades to nothing. Acoustic and organic throughout, real Anatolian
instrumentation, gentle dynamics, mid-tempo, one single melodic idea returning in three different
weights, mixed soft and wide so a narrator sits clearly on top. No vocals, no lyrics, no choir, no
electronic drums, no synth pads, no brass fanfare, no drop, no loud percussion hits.
