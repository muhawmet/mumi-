The camera cranes down about a foot toward the wet cobbles, parallel to the rug so its shape never skews, and the mirrored rug and both figures rise out of the reflection below; it stops the moment that reflection is fully in frame, halfway through, and holds dead still. At first the rug hangs in a deep sag between them, water running off its whole lower edge in an unbroken curtain, both leaning in over their own end. Then the curtain thins from the ends inward and breaks into a countable handful of separate drops, and the woman's gaze travels down the sag to the middle. Half a second later one heavy drop lets go from the centre of the red medallion, rings the puddle below, and the old man's eyes follow it down and he blinks once. By the end its rings have run into the rings around them and both are looking at the same point in the middle, where each was looking at their own end. The fringe stops swinging and hangs straight. Neither steps back, their feet do not move, their backs do not straighten, their arms do not extend, and the sag never pulls out or goes taut. Each keeps their own end: the same fingers stay on the same edge and the grips never change hands. Exactly two people are in frame throughout, the headscarfed woman on the left and the flat-capped old man on the right, and nobody enters between them. The rug keeps the same red medallion pattern, length, border and fringe. Silent clip, no audio, no dialogue, mouth closed, no lip movement. No whip-pan, no shake, no snap-zoom, no camera warp.
